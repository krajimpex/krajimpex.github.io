The craft

Cut for light,not for weight.

A fraction of a degree in the angle of a facet decides whether a stone glows or falls flat.

Crafted by light

A diamond is judged by one thing: what it does with light. Every facet is placed to take light in, turn it, and send it back to the eye.

That is why the house looks at the cut before the carat, and shows only stones that return light fully.

What the eye sees

Three ways a diamond answers light.

I

Brilliance

The white light a stone returns to the eye. It comes from facets angled so light is turned back up rather than lost through the bottom.

II

Fire

Flashes of spectral colour. The diamond splits white light into its colours, and a well-proportioned crown spreads them for the eye to see.

III

Scintillation

The sparkle as the stone, the light or the viewer moves: a pattern of bright and dark that changes with every turn.

From rough to record

Five hands, one standard.

  1. Planning

    The rough is mapped to find the stone inside it that will return the most light, not the one that keeps the most weight.

  2. Cleaving and sawing

    The crystal is divided along the plan, with the cut chosen to protect the colour and clarity the stone will have.

  3. Bruting

    The outline is shaped: round, oval, pear or step-cut, with the girdle set to the proportions of the plan.

  4. Polishing

    Facet by facet, angles are brought to within a fraction of a degree, then checked again under magnification.

  5. Grading

    Every finished stone goes to independent grading, and is declared as natural or laboratory-grown before it is shown.
